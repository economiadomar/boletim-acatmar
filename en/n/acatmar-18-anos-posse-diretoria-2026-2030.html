ACATMAR turns 18, re-elects Mané Ferrari and strengthens its national role in the sea economy

November 17, 2026
Members, board and council elected for the 2026-2030 term, aboard the Kattamaram IV, in Florianópolis.
Photo: Divulgação ACATMAR

Eighteen years of work, achievements that helped transform Brazil's nautical sector and a new stage of expansion in the sea economy. It was with this assessment, and an eye on the challenges ahead, that ACATMAR, the Brazilian Nautical Association, celebrated its anniversary on Tuesday night (17), aboard the Kattamaram IV, in Florianópolis. During the Ordinary General Assembly, members elected by acclamation the Board and the Council for the 2026-2030 term, returning Leandro "Mané" Ferrari Lobo to the presidency. Márcio Alexandre Evangelista remains as vice-president.

The new term marks the continuity of a path built on the defense of the nautical sector and reinforces a movement to broaden the association's representation. Active from north to south of the country, ACATMAR now further strengthens its presence across different segments of the sea economy, including fishing, mariculture, defense, shipbuilding and offshore, tourism and services.

On the same night, those responsible for the sector directorates and regional representations also took office. The structure widens the channels of dialogue with the different segments of the production chain, allowing companies, professionals and institutions to present demands, develop joint projects and take part in discussions with public authorities.

More than an administrative change, the new composition reflects the aim of bringing together different activities connected to Brazilian waters and turning the sector's needs into concrete initiatives.

From the pandemic recovery to the nautical sector's expansion

Among the milestones of ACATMAR's path is its role in resuming nautical activities during the Covid-19 pandemic. In a period marked by restrictions and uncertainty, the association worked with public authorities to design safety measures that would allow activities to gradually reopen.

The proposals presented by the association were first adopted in Santa Catarina. The experience led ACATMAR to expand its dialogue with the Federal Government, through the Ministry of Tourism, and the measures became a reference for other states.

The initiative helped create the conditions for the reopening of Brazil's nautical sector and preceded a period of strong market expansion. The episode became an example of the association's ability to connect the productive sector and the different levels of government.

Regularized marinas and new opportunities for municipalities

ACATMAR's work also helped address one of the historical challenges of Brazilian boating: regularizing and expanding infrastructure.

Through the Marina Legal Project, the association helped regularize marinas and other nautical structures, offering technical support with documentation and environmental licensing. The work advanced into the planning of new infrastructure. The association advised municipalities and designed and donated to states and city halls marina projects, waterfront interventions and nautical planning proposals, contributing to the organization of activities and to making the most of each region's economic potential.

Bringing communities closer to the nautical world was also part of this path. With the Circo Náutico and actions at festivals, ACATMAR took sports and leisure activities to municipalities with rivers, lakes and reservoirs.

These initiatives helped show that the opportunities of the sea economy are not limited to the coast. Inland waters also represent possibilities for developing tourism, sports, leisure and new businesses.

Work in Congress and international connections

Defending the sector's interests also led ACATMAR to expand its participation in discussions on public policy and legislation.

In 2025, the association supported the Senate in creating the Parliamentary Front for the Sea Economy – Nautical Sector, strengthening the presence of nautical issues in the national debate. Throughout its history, the association has worked with public authorities and the private sector on legislative proposals and on reviewing rules with a direct impact on the sector's activities.

Internationally, ACATMAR has promoted business missions, technical visits and business rounds, as well as signing cooperation agreements with government bodies and foreign organizations. One of these fronts is Blue Nautical HUB Brasil, an initiative by Mundo Mar Consulting with ACATMAR taking part in its delivery. The project brings together Brazilian and international companies and institutions to develop joint initiatives, establish partnerships and open new markets.

Professional training and innovation for the sea economy

Training professionals has also become one of the association's main fronts.

In October 2026, the ACATMAR Technical Training Forum reached its sixth edition, with 229 registered participants. The initiative updates knowledge and brings together professionals, specialists and companies working across the nautical chain.

Another step forward came in 2026, with a cooperation agreement between ACATMAR and the Federal Institute of Santa Catarina (IFSC) to support the implementation of the Marina Escola. The proposal is to create a space for professional training, applied research and innovation, helping to prepare workers and develop solutions for the challenges of the sea economy.

Limpeza dos Mares: 205 tonnes of waste removed and environmental education for 5,000 children

Preserving aquatic environments is another hallmark of the association's 18 years.

Carried out by ACATMAR in partnership with Acquanauta and Mundo Mar Consulting, the Limpeza dos Mares project had removed 205 tonnes of waste over 12 years of activity as of September 2026.

The work goes beyond removing litter from aquatic environments. The actions combine volunteer mobilization, awareness and environmental education. In 2026 alone, 5,000 children were reached by educational activities, bringing new generations closer to the importance of preserving the waters.

ACATMAR is also part of the National Jury of the Blue Flag Program, contributing to the assessment of applications for the environmental certification.

A new term expands the representation of the sea economy

As it turns 18, ACATMAR begins a new cycle with the challenge of consolidating its achievements and expanding its national reach. The 2026-2030 term will continue the work developed in boating while strengthening the representation of the entire sea economy production chain.

From fishing and mariculture to defense, shipbuilding and offshore, tourism and services, the aim is to bring the different segments closer together, strengthen institutional dialogue and take the demands of those who work in Brazilian waters to decision-making spaces.

The goal is to turn this representation into concrete projects, development opportunities and progress for the sectors that make up Brazil's blue economy.
